The problem

Two sensors. Two scales. One broken seam.

  • Aqua/MODIS and Suomi-NPP/VIIRS do not agree on what a “fire” is: different pixels, sensitivity and overpasses.
  • In February 2012 the record switches sensor. Naively joining the files creates an artificial jump.
  • Outage months are often treated as zero, which hides real activity and breaks comparisons.
  • A thermal hotspot is not a confirmed fire, and not burned area.

Harmonization

One scale, with the seam made explicit.

  • MODIS is mapped to the Suomi-NPP hotspot-day scale with a per-cell, per-block calibration model.
  • Satellite availability is derived from daily observations, not a static outage list: a month is only replaced when it really lacks most of its days.
  • Known limits: sparse absence means zero only inside published support; unknown months stay unknown.
  • Out-of-sample validation is shown per country. High-activity Brazil has ~6% CV WAPE; small, low-activity countries can be 40–55%, and the UI states the bias and sample size.

What makes it different

Built to answer real questions, fast.

  • Search any country or region; drill into states and departments.
  • Compare two places on one scale, or index each to its own peak to compare shapes.
  • Planetary Computer context: land cover and MODIS burned area, masked to the country.
  • Meteomatics weather at the selection centre for the chosen day.
  • Playback loops from cache; the API is protected against oversized grids and exports.

Impact and honesty

Useful for patterns. Not a fire counter.

  • For researchers, journalists, agencies and students: one comparable calendar instead of six disagreeing CSVs.
  • Designed for low bandwidth: static frontend, cached API JSON, no account, no backend on the page.
  • Never cite a hotspot as a confirmed fire or as burned area. The app keeps that caveat in the console and in the data.
